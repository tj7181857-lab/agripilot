Problem Analysis

Problem Statement & Practical Agriculture Context

Slide 02

Ground-Level Challenges in Indian Farming

  • Empirical vs. Scientific Sowing: Farmers predominantly select crops based on tradition rather than analytical matching with soil minerals (N, P, K, pH) and rainfall patterns.
  • Delayed Disease Diagnosis: Plant pathology expertise is scarce in rural clusters. Delayed diagnosis often leads to crop loss or indiscriminate chemical spraying.
  • Digital Language Barrier: Standard advisory tools and agricultural databases are primarily in English, excluding non-English-literate farmers.
  • Fragmented Record-Keeping: Farm history (soil reports, previous crop yields, disease outbreaks) is maintained informally, leading to lost context across seasons.

The Machine Learning Integration Gap

  • Standalone Black-Box Models: Existing computer vision models output a raw disease label without visual evidence, hindering farmer confidence and field verification.
  • Disconnected Soil & Vision Pipelines: Most tools address either soil or leaf photos independently without linking them to an integrated farm management profile.
  • Requirement for Real-World Feasibility: Models must be lightweight enough to perform sub-second inference without expensive cloud hardware.
Formal Mid-Sem Problem Statement: "To design and evaluate an accessible machine learning system that provides scientific crop recommendation from soil-weather data, interpretable leaf disease classification via Grad-CAM, and persistent, multilingual farm record management for individual farmers."
Project Scope

Project Objectives: ML vs. Application Systems

Slide 03

Machine Learning Objectives

  • Soil-Centric Crop Recommendation: Benchmark Decision Tree, Random Forest, and Gradient Boosting algorithms on 7 soil & climate parameters across 22 crop classes.
  • Deep Disease Classification: Build and fine-tune a convolutional transfer learning pipeline (MobileNetV2) for crop leaf disease detection.
  • Interpretability via Grad-CAM: Implement Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping to visually highlight disease lesions on leaf images.
  • Model Artifact Persistence: Serialize and bundle models (.joblib, .keras) and offline evaluation metrics for instant inference.

Application & System Objectives

  • Isolated Multi-User Profiles: Enforce session authorization in SQLite so each farmer exclusively manages their own farm plots, crop cycles, and scan history.
  • Tri-Lingual Localization: Provide native interface and advisory support in English, Hindi (हिंदी), and Marathi (मराठी).
  • Analyst Dashboard: Provide a dedicated analyst view exposing project dataset aggregates, confusion matrices, and saved ML metrics.
  • Contextual Farm Advisory: Provide actionable recommendations using weather context without ever fabricating missing laboratory soil values.

Data Engineering

Datasets & Preprocessing Pipelines

Slide 05

Dataset 1: Crop Recommendation (Tabular)

  • Sample Volume: 2,200 verified agricultural data points.
  • Split: 1,760 train samples (80%) : 440 test samples (20%).
  • 7 Input Features:
    • Soil: Nitrogen ($N$), Phosphorus ($P$), Potassium ($K$)
    • Climatic: Temperature, Relative Humidity, Rainfall
    • Chemical: Soil pH level
  • 22 Target Crop Classes: Rice, Maize, Chickpea, Kidneybeans, Pigeonpeas, Mothbeans, Mungbean, Blackgram, Lentil, Pomegranate, Banana, Mango, Grapes, Watermelon, Muskmelon, Apple, Orange, Papaya, Coconut, Cotton, Jute, Coffee.
  • Preprocessing: Range checks, null imputation check, stratified train/test split (seed=42).

Dataset 2: Crop Leaf Pathology (Vision)

  • Image Modality: Standardized RGB leaf photos ($224 \times 224 \times 3$).
  • Sample Volume: 330 curated images across held-out splits.
  • Split: 264 train images (80%) : 66 held-out test images (20%).
  • 11 Classes Across 3 Supported Crops:
    • Grape: Black rot, Esca (Black Measles), Leaf blight, Healthy
    • Potato: Early blight, Late blight, Healthy
    • Tomato: Bacterial spot, Early blight, Late blight, Healthy
  • Preprocessing: Mini-batch tensor loading, pixel rescaling to $[0, 1]$, and bilinear interpolation.
Dataset Scope Boundary: System predictions are strictly bounded by these 22 crop types and 11 pathology classes. Out-of-scope crops and diseases cannot be recognized at this stage.

System Implementation

Application Workflow & Farmer Usability

Slide 08

Farmer Interaction Journey

[New Farmer] [Returning Farmer] ↓ ↓ [Register & Select Language] [Authenticate Login] ↓ ↓ [Farm Setup: Area, Soil Type, Irrigation] [Load Farm Profile & Past Logs] ↓ ↓ ------------------ CHOOSE ADVISORY SERVICE ------------------ ↓ ↓ [Crop Recommendation] [Leaf Disease Scanner] - Input available soil test data - Upload leaf image - Missing N-P-K handled transparently - MobileNetV2 prediction - Top ranked crop recommendation - Grad-CAM heatmap visualization ↓ ↓ ----------------- PERSIST TO SQLITE DATABASE ----------------- ↓ [Farmer History Portal: View Own Past Scans & Seasonal Advice]

Farmer-Centric Engineering Principles

  • No Artificial Soil Chemistry: If a farmer lacks a laboratory soil test report, the system does not invent artificial N-P-K numbers; it flags missing data transparently.
  • Strict Tenant Isolation: User sessions enforce foreign-key boundaries in SQLite (user_id matching). A farmer never accesses another farmer's plots or scan history.
  • Tri-Lingual Localization: Dynamic translation of advisory content into English, Hindi (हिंदी), and Marathi (मराठी) to bridge rural literacy gaps.
  • Contextual Weather Integration: Sowing advisories leverage local climatic conditions alongside pure machine learning classification.
Critical Appraisal

Current Limitations & Technical Challenges

Slide 09

1. Domain Shift in Field Photography

Held-out benchmark accuracy (87.88%) degrades under uncontrolled field conditions: direct solar glare, shadows, muddy leaf residues, and low-resolution phone cameras.

2. Bounded Crop & Pathology Taxonomy

The vision model recognizes exactly 11 states across 3 crops (Grape, Potato, Tomato). Crucial regional crops (Cotton, Sugarcane, Soybean) are not yet represented.

3. Lack of Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Gating

Uploading an arbitrary non-leaf image (soil, background, non-crop objects) produces a forced classification among the 11 classes rather than a graceful rejection.

4. Manual Soil Data Entry Friction

Farmers without digital soil cards must manually transcribe numerical N-P-K parameters; automated OCR extraction from photographed cards is not yet built.

Mid-Semester Reflection: "These limitations reflect authentic developmental milestones in an engineering project. Recognizing these failure modes provides the concrete technical requirements for our end-semester completion."
Roadmap

Future Work & End-Semester Project Roadmap

Slide 10

Phase 1: Robustness & Data

  • Field Data Expansion: Augment image dataset with unconstrained field photos showing complex backgrounds and varied lighting.
  • Synthetic Augmentations: Apply contrast jitter, motion blur, and perspective transforms during retraining.
  • Expand Crop Scope: Add regional staple crops (Cotton, Rice, Soybean).

Phase 2: OOD & Automation

  • OOD Novelty Filter: Implement softmax thresholding and background rejection to filter invalid uploads.
  • Soil Card OCR: Build an automated parsing pipeline (Tesseract/EasyOCR) to read physical Soil Health Cards.
  • Model Quantization: Convert weights to TFLite for fast edge mobile deployment.

Phase 3: Advisory & Pilot

  • Agricultural Knowledge Base: Deepen contextual guidance with local agro-advisory guidelines.
  • Enhanced Dialect Support: Refine Marathi and Hindi agricultural terminologies.
  • Pilot Usability Testing: Conduct field validation with local farming samples in Maharashtra.
Current Project Status: "Core machine learning pipelines (tabular crop recommendation + MobileNetV2 disease detection + Grad-CAM) and multi-tenant web application are functional. Further robustness, OOD validation, and usability improvements are scheduled for the end-semester release."
